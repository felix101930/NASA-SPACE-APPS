# How common are these problems? (researched 2026-11-14, primary or high-quality sources)

## Lunar landing attempts since 2019
- **Counting rule:** a mission counts once it began powered descent to the surface.
  - Peregrine (propulsion leak, never attempted a landing) is excluded.
  - Luna 25 (crashed during an orbit burn) is excluded.
  - No attempts have happened in 2026 so far. Re-check before presenting, because Griffin may launch in late November.

- **Overall: 12 attempts, 5 upright.**
- **Commercial landers only:** 6 attempts, 1 upright.
- **Height or range measurement** was involved in 4 of the 7 failures: Hakuto-R M1, IM-1, IM-2 and Hakuto-R M2.

## The same hazards in other missions (quotes)
- **Apollo 11:** Armstrong flew manually "beyond the boulder field surrounding West Crater" (ALSJ).
- **Apollo 15:** "two of its landing pads were just over the edge of a small crater that Scott had not been able to see" (NASA SP-4214). The crew also reported "very few shadows … made themselves think the terrain had more topographic relief than it really did" (ALSJ).
- **Apollo 12:** at a 7.7° sun, the view away from the Sun was "washed-up … all but impossible to see detail" (ALSJ).
- **Apollo 14:** the crew "got within about 30 meters of the rim, without ever recognizing it" (ALSJ).
- **NASA on the south pole:**
  - "The Sun will hover just above the horizon, casting long, dark shadows" (NASA).
  - "shadows behind a lander could extend for miles" (NASA HLS lighting simulation).
  - The JETT3 rehearsals "occur at night, and a simulated sun will produce lighting and shadows" (NASA).
- **Ingenuity:** it "couldn't find enough surface features to track" over "steep, relatively featureless sand ripples". This is NASA's most likely explanation (NASA/JPL, Dec 2024).
- **Spirit (2009) and Opportunity (2005):** both were caught in soft soil or sand (NASA).
- **Sensing faults, NOT terrain:** Schiaparelli (2016) and Mars Polar Lander (1999).

## Hook facts (stage-safe)
1. "Since 2019, 12 robotic landers began descent to the Moon; only 5 came to rest upright."
2. "In four of the seven failures, the lander misjudged or couldn't measure its height. One rejected a real 3 km crater-rim drop as sensor error."
3. "Intuitive Machines said low-angle south-pole sunlight 'created long shadows and dim lighting conditions' that contributed to its 2025 lander tipping over."
4. "NASA's Ingenuity helicopter crashed after its navigation 'couldn't find enough surface features to track' over featureless sand." (NASA's most likely explanation)
5. "Apollo 14 astronauts came within about 30 m of Cone Crater's rim without recognizing it."

## Do not claim
- That IM-2 failed from lighting alone.
- That SLIM landed "upside-down" as JAXA's wording.
- That Luna 25, Beresheet, Vikram or SLIM were terrain failures.
- That Peregrine attempted a landing.
- Any 2026 landing (re-check first).
- An Apollo 15 tilt angle.
- That Ingenuity's cause is confirmed.
- That Schiaparelli or Mars Polar Lander were fooled by terrain.
- "Artemis III will land" (Artemis III is no longer a landing; say "Artemis crews").
- That Chandrayaan-3 landed "at the pole" (it landed at 69.3°S).
